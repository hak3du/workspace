   308→
   309→[jetforge.io](https://jetforge.io/)
   310→
   311→Launch Arena connects the social and creator experience to real digital asset infrastructure.
   312→
   313→The goal is to make project discovery and launch participation accessible while clearly presenting the risks and mechanics of digital assets.
   314→
   315→## Launch discovery
   316→
   317→Users can browse projects by launch status, category, launch time, liquidity, trading activity, and other available market data.
   318→
   319→Each project page should show, where applicable:
   320→
   321→- Project name, symbol, logo, and media.
   322→- Creator identity and project Space.
   323→- Token mint address and relevant program addresses.
   324→- Network and launch status.
   325→- Supply and allocation details.
   326→- Liquidity and trading information from a supported data source.
   327→- Transaction history and relevant on-chain links.
   328→- Launch requirements and eligibility.
   329→- Security checks, their sources, and their limitations.
   330→- Fees and risks before a user signs a transaction.
   331→
   332→A risk indicator must explain its basis. For example, an unverified project owner, suspicious address pattern, or missing metadata may justify a warning, but a heuristic is not definitive proof of fraud.
   333→
   334→## Creating a launch
   335→
   336→The launch workflow should be explicit:
   337→
   338→1. The creator opens the launch builder.
   339→2. They enter project metadata and upload approved media.
   340→3. They select a supported launch configuration.
   341→4. The backend validates inputs and permissions.
   342→5. The application displays the expected network cost and platform fee.
   343→6. The wallet prepares and signs the required transaction.
   344→7. The platform submits or observes the transaction through its supported integration.
   345→8. The backend verifies the actual on-chain outcome.
   346→9. The launch page is updated only when the relevant transaction and launch state are confirmed.
   347→10. A receipt records the verified outcome and itemized charges.
   348→
   349→A failed or expired transaction must not be recorded as a successful launch. Retries must not create duplicate platform charges.
   350→
   351→Important: real token deployment requires a clearly specified Solana program or launch provider. Wallet connection alone cannot create a token-launch mechanism. We must implement or integrate the actual launch transaction and verify its behavior before mainnet use.
   352→
   353→## Wallet integration
   354→
   355→Supported wallets should connect through standard wallet interfaces, including Phantom and Solflare where compatible.
   356→
   357→The app should:
   358→
   359→- Detect available wallets.
   360→- Request explicit connection approval.
   361→- Display the connected public address.
   362→- Show balances and network status when supported.
   363→- Reconnect where the wallet permits it.
   364→- Request signed authentication messages when backend verification is needed.
   365→- Display the exact transaction and fee before signing.
   366→- Verify confirmed transactions before granting credits or recording revenue.
   367→- Handle rejected signatures, insufficient balances, RPC errors, and network changes.
   368→
   369→The application must never request seed phrases or private keys. It should not be able to silently sign transactions.
   370→
   371→## Market terminal
   372→
   373→Launch Arena also provides an optional market terminal with price charts, trading volume, liquidity, transaction history, token metadata, watchlists, and alerts.
   374→
   375→Data sources should be named, timestamps should be visible where useful, and stale or unavailable data should be clearly indicated.
   376→
   377→Trading and swap functionality should only be offered through an actual supported transaction route. The interface must not imply that the platform collects a percentage of every market trade unless the relevant route technically enforces that fee.

   379→# 8. The revenue and payment engine
   380→
   381→The monetization system must be built as infrastructure, not as a visual badge or a number in a database.
   382→
   383→## Revenue streams
   384→
   385→| Product                | Revenue mechanism                                       |
   386→| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
   387→| Launch services        | Configurable fee on supported successful launches       |
   388→| AHE API                | Usage-based requests, credits, and paid service tiers   |
   389→| Creator economy        | Optional subscriptions and paid content                 |
   390→| Premium Spaces         | Additional administration, analytics, and customization |
   391→| Developer platform     | API quotas, integration features, and enterprise plans  |
   392→| Supported transactions | Disclosed fee on eligible transactions where enforced   |
   393→
   394→An illustrative launch fee such as 0.01 SOL can be evaluated as a configurable starting point, but it is not a live fee until the treasury, transaction logic, and fee accounting are implemented and tested.
   395→
   396→## Fee engine requirements
   397→
   398→Each fee should have:
   399→
   400→- A fee identifier and version.
   401→- A defined payer and recipient.
   402→- A clear calculation rule.
   403→- A currency and exact amount.
   404→- A transaction or payment reference.
   405→- A lifecycle status.
   406→- A verified settlement record.
   407→- A reconciliation mechanism.
   408→
   409→The system must prevent duplicate charges, distinguish pending payments from confirmed ones, and reconcile its internal records against the actual payment provider or blockchain.
   410→
   411→Revenue dashboards should distinguish quoted fees, submitted payments, confirmed payments, refunds, and net revenue.
   412→
   413→A fee ledger is an accounting record, not a substitute for the actual payment. It must never declare revenue received before settlement is verified.

   423→## AHE: Adaptive Hashing Encryption
   424→
   425→AHE should be integrated as a dedicated cryptographic service based on your existing `AHE_SDK` repository, rather than recreated inside browser code or blindly ported into the frontend.
   426→
   427→Its intended capabilities include:
   428→
   429→- Versioned encryption and decryption APIs.
   430→- Authenticated encryption and tamper detection.
   431→- Post-quantum key establishment where supported by the validated protocol.
   432→- Key derivation and cryptographic context binding.
   433→- Key and algorithm version management.
   434→- Request authentication and usage metering.
   435→- Health checks and operational monitoring.
   436→- Developer documentation, examples, and test environments.
   437→- Integration with paid API plans.
   438→
   439→A reasonable architecture to validate against the actual SDK is ML-KEM for post-quantum key establishment, HKDF-SHA-256 for deriving encryption keys, and AES-256-GCM for authenticated encryption. The exact design must follow a documented protocol and tested implementation.
   440→
   441→### AHE's operating principles
   442→
   443→1. Never return private key material or raw shared secrets to the browser.
   444→2. Never log plaintext, private keys, or derived encryption secrets.
   445→3. Use unique, correctly managed nonces for AES-GCM.
   446→4. Authenticate requesters and enforce quotas.
   447→5. Version algorithms and ciphertext formats.
   448→6. Test modified ciphertexts, incorrect keys, invalid parameters, replay attempts where relevant, and failure paths.
   449→7. Define key rotation, revocation, retention, and recovery procedures.
   450→
   451→AHE should be presented as a cryptographic product whose properties are established by implementation and testing. Post-quantum components alone do not make an entire system quantum-safe, and the product should not claim independent certification without evidence.
   452→
   453→## AHE developer console
   454→
   455→Developers need a working environment to:
   456→
   457→- Create and revoke API keys.
   458→- Read API documentation.
   459→- Inspect request and response schemas.
   460→- Run sample encrypt/decrypt operations using test data.
   461→- View latency, usage, quotas, and errors.
   462→- See algorithm and protocol versions.
   463→- Manage plans and billing.
   464→- Access SDK examples and integration guides.
   465→
   466→The console should clearly separate sandbox and production environments. Test keys and sample ciphertexts must never be treated as production secrets.
   467→
   468→## PQC.market-inspired integration
   469→
   470→PQC.market is a technical reference for post-quantum launch attestations and related protocol concepts. The integration must be based on its actual published API surface, license and terms, supported methods, and compatibility.
   471→
   472→The architecture should use an adapter so the launchpad can call the provider or verified implementation without coupling the whole product to it.
   473→
   474→The adapter must establish:
   475→
   476→- Which endpoints or SDKs are actually supported.
   477→- How requests are authenticated, if authentication is required.
   478→- How launch payloads are canonicalized.
   479→- Which signature and verification algorithms are used.
   480→- How key state and one-time signing material are managed.
   481→- How failures and unavailable services are handled.
   482→- How the result is independently verified and displayed.
   483→
   484→If no supported API exists for a desired function, we must not invent an endpoint or claim a successful integration. We can implement a permitted, independently reviewed protocol adapter where appropriate.
   485→
   486→### What a launch attestation means
   487→
   488→A valid attestation may establish that specified launch metadata was signed under a particular protocol and key.
   489→
   490→It does not automatically establish that a project is honest, its smart contracts are safe, or its ordinary Solana transactions are resistant to quantum attacks.
   491→
   492→Solana's normal signature and execution environment must be evaluated separately. A post-quantum attestation layer does not replace the chain's transaction-signature scheme.
   493→

   502→- Repository and release links.
   503→- Available contract or program checks.

   729→
   730→The product is not ready for real financial activity merely because the UI works.
   731→
   732→Before mainnet payments or public launches are enabled, the release must pass these gates:
   733→
   734→- The application builds and its tests pass.
   735→- Authentication and permissions are enforced server-side.
   736→- Wallet ownership verification works.
   737→- Supported launch transactions are tested on Solana devnet.
   738→- The actual fee recipient and amount are verified.
   739→- Duplicate payments and retry cases are tested.
   740→- On-chain confirmation and reconciliation work.
   741→- AHE cryptographic operations pass appropriate security tests.
   742→- Attestation verification matches the actual protocol.
   743→- Media uploads and private Space permissions are secure.
   744→- Secrets, monitoring, backups, and rollback procedures are configured.
   745→- Terms, privacy, fees, and relevant risk disclosures are in place.
   746→- Production credentials are separated from development credentials.
   747→
   748→Standards such as NIST guidance can inform engineering and risk controls, but the platform must not claim NIST or ISO certification unless it has actually completed the relevant assessment and obtained the applicable certification.

   828→# 21. The build strategy
   829→
   830→To build this properly, I would divide V2.5 into four delivery stages while keeping the final architecture consistent.
   831→
   832→Stage 1 — Product foundation: design system, application shell, authentication, profiles, media publishing, Spaces, and discovery.
   833→
   834→Stage 2 — Real transactions: Solana wallet connectivity, wallet ownership verification, supported launch flow, fee quotes, on-chain verification, and payment accounting.
   835→
   836→Stage 3 — Technical differentiation: AHE integration, PQC protocol adapter, developer console, GitHub links, API metering, and security verification.
   837→
   838→Stage 4 — Full ecosystem: messaging, live experiences, premium plans, creator monetization, AI guide, advanced analytics, administrative tools, and production hardening.
   839→
   840→The ordering is intentional. We build a useful product early, prove the revenue path, and then expand the ecosystem without pretending unimplemented services are live.
